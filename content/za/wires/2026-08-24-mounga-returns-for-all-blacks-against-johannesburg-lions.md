Fly-half Richie Mo'unga will make his first appearance for New Zealand since the 2023 Rugby World Cup when they face the Lions franchise in Johannesburg on Tuesday.</p><p>After the global showpiece in France three years ago, Mo'unga joined Japanese club Brave Lupus, rendering him ineligible to represent his country.</p><p>The 32-year-old playmaker, who kicked two penalties in the 12-11 loss to South Africa in the World Cup final, recently returned to New Zealand. </p><p>All Blacks teams are chosen exclusively from players within New Zealand, unlike South Africa, many of whose first choices are based in Japan.</p><p>Mo'unga is among six replacements called up by head coach Dave Rennie during the tour due to injuries. He came in for centre Billy Proctor.</p><p>None of the All Blacks starters in the 33-16 triumph over the Springboks in the first Test on Saturday will face the Lions. </p><p>But three of the Test replacements -- centre Anton Lienert-Brown, scrum-half Kyle Preston and loose forward Peter Lakai -- sit on the bench again.</p><p>Assuming he comes off the bench, Preston will be the only All Black to play in the first five of the eight tour matches. </p><p>Prop Ofa Tu'ungafasi and lock Jamie Hannah, who were called up to provide cover for first Test concussion casualties Ethan de Groot and Josh Lord, are also among the replacements. </p><p>The other replacement prop, Saula Ma'u, is set to make his first appearance for three-time World Cup winners New Zealand. </p><p>"I want to congratulate Saula.
