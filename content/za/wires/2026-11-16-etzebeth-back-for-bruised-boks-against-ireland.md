Etzebeth back for bruised Boks against Ireland Vusi. Bafetane Mon, 11/16/2026 - 17:51 DUBLIN - South Africa rugby legend Eben Etzebeth returns to the starting XV for the world champions' Nations Championship Test against Ireland this weekend with coach Rassie Erasmus saying on Monday they are "determined to fix the mistakes that cost us" in the defeat by France. The Springboks lie second in the southern hemisphere standings, a point adrift of New Zealand who were beaten by Wales for the first time in 73 years, after they lost 21-19 in a pulsating match in Paris last Friday. A South Africa win over Ireland and England beating the All Blacks next Saturday would be enough for Erasmus's side to feature in the top place final on November 29 at Twickenham. "We let ourselves and the country down, and we are determined to fix the mistakes that cost us," he said in a South Africa Rugby statement. "So we'll leave no stone unturned to ensure that we play according to our standards this weekend. "The coaches and the entire squad took a good look at ourselves over the last few days and we know what we did wrong and what we have to improve on, so we know what we have to do this week." - 'A quality team' - Erasmus made five changes in all to the starting line-up that faced the French, who gained a measure of revenge for their World Cup quarter-final defeat on the same ground in 2023. Etzebeth, who will extend his record as the most capped Springbok in history to 148, will link up with Ruan Nortje in the engine room. The 35-year-old had been ruled out of the tour originally because of injury but recovered sufficiently to be called up earlier this month. The other changes to the starting team are Cameron Hanekom joining forces with captain Siya Kolisi, and Pieter-Steph du Toit in the back row whilst Ox Nche, Malcolm Marx, and Wilco Louw return to the front row. Erasmus made a further seven changes on the replacements bench, which again feature six forwards and two backs, although four of those players featured in the matchday squad against France. Two players who do come onto the replacements bench having sat out the French game are Grant Williams as scrum-half cover in place of Cobus Reinach, while Canan Moodie comes in for Manie Libbok. "We also think what Grant and Canan offer will suit us in terms of what we want to achieve against Ireland," said Erasmus. South Africa are seeking their second successive win in Dublin, having won for the first time there in 13 years last November. Ireland have had the better of the Springboks in recent years, winning four of their last six meetings, including being the only side to beat them at the 2023 World Cup -- in the pool stage. "They are a quality team, who always give everything against us, so it's going to take an accurate 80-minute performance if we want to get the result against them," said Erasmus. "They have classy forwards and backs, and they will give everything knowing that they are still in the running for a place in the final of the competition next weekend with England and France to finish the league stages as the top northern hemisphere team." Erasmus' Ireland counterpart Andy Farrell will name his side on Thursday. Team (15-1) Damian Willemse; Kurt-Lee Arendse, Jesse Kriel, Damian de Allende, Ethan Hooker; Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu, Morne van den Berg; Cameron Hanekom, Pieter-Steph du Toit, Siya Kolisi (capt); Ruan Nortje, Eben Etzebeth; Wilco Louw, Malcolm Marx, Ox Nche Replacements: Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp, Thomas du Toit, Lood de Jager, Paul de Villiers, Marco van Staden, Grant Williams, Canan Moodie Coach: Rassie Erasmus (RSA)
