South Africa’s water crisis is no longer just about dry taps, but what happens when emergency measures become a permanent business model. As municipalities spend hundreds of millions on water tankers, people remain trapped without reliable infrastructure. The question is no longer whether tankers are necessary, but who benefits when the emergency never ends and how long South Africans can afford to pay for failure.
