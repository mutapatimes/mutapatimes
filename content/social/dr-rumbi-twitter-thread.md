**1/** Meet Dr Rumbi.

A Zimbabwean-British doctor who wants to take what the West knows about medicine and carry it home to the women who need it. We spent a cool July afternoon with her at the Wellcome Collection. 🧵
[attach: feed-1.png cover]

**2/** She is a practising NHS doctor working in general practice, an educator, and the founder and CEO of Glacie Health, a women's-health venture built to close the gap between good intentions and the care women actually receive.

**3/** Her thesis: education is the medicine.

"If I don't know I'm meant to check my breasts, how would I ever know I had a lump?"

Care you were never taught to recognise is care you can never reach for.

**4/** What Glacie Health does, in one line: close the gap between intention and measurable impact, so care is designed around women's real needs rather than assumed for them.
[attach: About Glacie green slide]

**5/** One project close to home: the Sally Mugabe Fertility Clinic in Harare. Reactivated. Low-cost, science-based fertility care for women who would otherwise spend years and thousands of dollars searching elsewhere.
[attach: Sally Mugabe green slide]

**6/** The point the clinic makes, and Glacie builds into every campaign: infertility is not a woman's problem alone. In roughly half of couples, the man is a factor.

**7/** On the brain drain, she flips the script:

"When you've trained here and you go back, there is an advantage waiting for you. It is, quite literally, your competitive advantage."

Nobody loses when expertise comes home.

**8/** On Black maternal health: Black women are around five times more likely to die in childbirth than white women. She sat on the panel that reported on it. The hardest part, she says, is that nobody comes to work intending harm.

**9/** And on risk, standing at the anatomical models:

"It makes nothing feel like too big a risk. A bit of embarrassment, a failure, who cares? We're all going to die anyway. Take the risk."
