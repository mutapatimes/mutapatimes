HARARE, 20 August 2026, By the Mutapa Times Sports Desk

ZIFA on Thursday ended four months of drift at the top of Zimbabwean football, unveiling 65-year-old Spaniard José Ramón Catalá Peiró as head coach of the Warriors and wrapping the appointment inside the most sweeping restructuring of the national technical setup in years.

Catalá, a UEFA Pro Licence holder, was presented in Harare alongside Croatian Stella Gotal, who becomes the first foreign coach to take charge of the Mighty Warriors. Between them they head a cast of eighteen appointments running from Under-15 to senior level on both the men's and women's sides, an announcement ZIFA president Nqobile Magwizi framed less as a hiring than as a doctrine.

"Today is not simply about announcing coaches," the association said in its statement. "It is about announcing a new technical direction for Zimbabwean football."

## The Spanish bloc

Catalá will be assisted by two compatriots, Marc Sánchez and Vicente Girona Izquierdo, and by local coach Takesure Chiragwi. Sánchez, whose background takes in CE Sabadell, CF Damm, Villarreal and Mallorca, doubles as head coach of the men's Under-23s, a deliberate stitching of the two squads together. Girona has worked at Villarreal, Shanghai Shenhua and with the Algerian Football Federation.

Former ZIFA technical director Wilson Mutekede has been named assistant to the Under-23 side, which the association describes as "a critical bridge between youth and senior international football."

Kaitano Tembo, who has held the Warriors together as interim coach since April, steps into the men's Under-20 job with former Dynamos and Warriors forward Max Makanza as his assistant. Tafadzwa Mashiri takes the Under-17s with George Mbwando alongside him, while Backlyfield Chivenga leads the Under-15s, assisted by Alexio Sigion.

On the women's side, former Mighty Warriors captain and coach Rosemary Mugadza takes the Under-20s with Naison Muchekela; Jatson Maphosa and Thubaelihle Sibanda get the Under-17s; and Irene Mwanza, with Jacob Chavungama, the Under-15s.

Gotal, also a UEFA Pro Licence holder, arrives from Gibraltar's senior women's national team and has previously worked inside Croatia's women's structures and Saudi Arabia's women's programme. She succeeds Sithethelelwe "Kwinji 15" Sibanda.

## Dream 2030

The organising idea is a programme ZIFA has christened Dream 2030, which the association says is meant to end the habit of assembling national teams competition by competition.

"For too long, our national teams have largely operated as separate entities: appointed at different times, prepared for individual competitions and often without enough connection from one age group to the next. We are changing that," ZIFA said.

The pathway, as the association puts it, is blunt to the point of slogan: "U15 identifies. U17 develops. U20 prepares. U23 transitions. The senior team performs."

Notably, ZIFA says youth coaches will not be judged on tournament results alone, but on how many players they push upwards. "The greatest achievement of an U17 coach may not simply be winning an U17 tournament," the statement read. "It may be producing six or seven future Warriors or Mighty Warriors."

Asked why the association reached for Spanish technical leadership, ZIFA pointed to Spain's development system rather than its trophy cabinet. "We are not trying to turn Zimbabwe into Spain. We want to combine Zimbabwean talent with world-class methodology and build a distinctly Zimbabwean football identity."

Catalá, for his part, said he was ready to embrace the responsibility of leading Zimbabwe and to work with the country's football structures to build a Warriors side capable of competing with the best on the continent.

## What the CV actually shows

ZIFA credits its new man with more than three decades across Europe, Africa, Asia and the Middle East, citing "previous roles involving Celta Vigo, Espanyol, Real Zaragoza and Dynamo Kyiv," and adding that he "has also coached Nicaragua and held technical roles with Libya."

Each of those clubs does appear in his history. The nature of the involvement is worth stating plainly.

At Celta Vigo in the early 1990s, and later at Osasuna, Lleida, Salamanca, Rayo Vallecano and Real Zaragoza, Catalá worked as a physical trainer, following coach Txetxu Rojo between jobs. At Espanyol, where he spent seven years from 2004, he was physical trainer and head of the club's research and planning department, serving under managers including Ernesto Valverde and Mauricio Pochettino. He later worked as a physical trainer with the Catalonia representative side under Johan Cruyff.

His Nicaragua spell, around 2012–13, was as an assistant. So was his first involvement with Libya, under the Spaniard Javier Clemente. At Dynamo Kyiv, from 2019, his title was academy director, a role he speaks about with evident pride, telling one coaching congress that he likes "teams that attack, that are brave, that look for the victory from the first moment."

His record as a senior international head coach, however, runs to two matches. Appointed Libya's interim manager in March 2022 after Clemente's departure by mutual consent, he oversaw a win over Niger and a defeat to Mauritania at a friendly tournament before the caretaker spell ended in April. He had been Libya's technical director since 2021.

That matters because ZIFA's own advertisement for the post, which closed on 30 October 2025, asked for "a minimum of 5 years' professional coaching experience at national team and/or top-tier club level" and "demonstrable experience in African competitions." Neither ZIFA nor Catalá addressed the point on Thursday. The association did not disclose the length of his contract or his salary.

## The clock is already running

Whatever the merits of a ten-year vision, Catalá's first assessment arrives in under five weeks.

The Warriors sit in Group E of qualifying for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations alongside DR Congo, Equatorial Guinea and Sierra Leone, with the opening two matchdays falling between 21 September and 6 October. Zimbabwe travel to Sierra Leone before hosting the Congolese in Harare.

The last permanent occupant of the office, Romanian Mario Marinică, lasted seven matches and five months, resigning on 27 April after taking the Warriors to the 2025 Nations Cup in Morocco and winning the Mukuru Four Nations tournament in Francistown.

ZIFA says Dream 2030 begins at Under-15, not with the senior team. Zimbabwean football's more immediate habit is to begin, and end, with results in September.
